# Protein validation and externally nominated labels

The validation studies answer separate questions: random-label search calibration, recovery of known generating axes, function-preserving basis changes, repeated-structure exposure labels, fixed-budget use, disulfide annotation and protein-level natural-language labels. None changes the original coordinate or data split after observing the corresponding endpoint.

Random-label trials use one fixed non-cysteine same-amino-acid pair in each of 47 groups, split into 23 development and 24 confirmation groups. Each search covers 1280 axes and two signs. One thousand trials compare unadjusted selection, calibration against 1999 independent maximum searches and testing a development-locked axis. Known single- and eight-axis generators use 500 trials each. Confirming a selected axis is distinct from recovering a generating member and sign.

Known-basis tests distribute neuron 814 over one to sixteen orthogonal directions while compensating the following linear projection. Sparse recovery is blind to the planted mapping; numerical equivalence of supplied write values is distinct from estimating a replacement. Repeated structures hold sequences, sites and predictions fixed and remeasure the structural labels. Fixed-budget allocation reranks whole bootstrap samples and uses the analytic expected random allocation loss.

Published disulfide neuron 361 and feature 4181 use the original indices and poles. Structural observed-residue, within-cysteine and local-sequence increments are separate. Protein-level natural-language explanations have whole-protein scoring and are not residue-level ground truth. All unfavourable increments and wide intervals remain in the results.

The original source byte digests, file sizes, preserved freeze timestamps and the corresponding English protocol are listed in frozen_records.json. Date stamps identify freezes; they do not denote separate reproduction outputs.

The separate fixed-annotation comparison fits group-weighted local sequence predictors in five family folds over all 47 groups (74 chains), evaluates each fixed published readout out of fold, and reports Brier gain with simultaneous 97.5 percent intervals for the two fixed-readout comparisons. This all-group analysis and the held-out 15-group assay reuse the same structural dataset and are not independent replications.
